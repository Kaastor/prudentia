# prudentia

This product is built on a Deweyan and Aristotelian approach to assignment design.

It is Deweyan because it treats assignments as structured experiences for inquiry, experimentation, consequence, and reflection. A good assignment is not merely a request for an answer. It is a designed situation in which students inspect, test, judge, revise, and explain what remains uncertain.

It is Aristotelian because it treats teaching as an exercise of practical wisdom. The teacher’s role is not simply to write tasks, but to choose the right challenge, the right evidence surface, the right scaffold, and the right level of difficulty for these learners, at this moment, for this purpose.

In the AI era, this matters because students can produce fluent answers more easily than ever. The central educational question is no longer only, “Can the student answer?” but also, “Can the student judge what to trust?” This approach does not ask teachers to ban AI, nor does it ask students to pretend AI was not used. Instead, it designs assignments in which AI use is permitted, but student judgment must become visible.

Students work under conditions that expose reasoning. What evidence supports this answer? What changed after revision? Which explanation should be rejected? What does this check prove, and what does it still leave open?

A strong assignment should unsettle easy answers, create room for experimentation, and require students to distinguish fluency from understanding. Its aim is not process purity, but evidence-bearing work: students inspect, compare, revise, and state limits.

This philosophy combines reflective experience, disciplined questioning, calibrated challenge, and evidence-based judgment. Its purpose is to help teachers design assignments in which students can use AI openly while still demonstrating the human capabilities that matter most: discernment, reasoning, revision, and responsible judgment.
